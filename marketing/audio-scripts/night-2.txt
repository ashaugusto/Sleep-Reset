Welcome to Night 2 of the Cognitive Shutdown Method. Tonight is about the shutdown ritual, the transition between the wired world and the resting brain.

The science is unambiguous. Screen light, especially in the blue-wavelength range, suppresses melatonin production by up to fifty percent for several hours after exposure. Melatonin is the chemical signal that tells your brain darkness is coming. Without it, your body does not know it is time to sleep.

But the issue is deeper than light. Phones, tablets, laptops, and television keep your prefrontal cortex active, processing information, evaluating, comparing, scrolling. That is the opposite of what your brain needs to fall asleep. Sleep requires the prefrontal cortex to disengage.

So tonight, you create a sixty-minute digital curfew. Sixty minutes before your intended bedtime, all screens go away. Phone in another room, on airplane mode, charging in the kitchen. Not on your nightstand. Not under your pillow. Out of reach.

In that sixty-minute window, choose one quiet activity. Reading a physical book. Light stretching. A warm shower. Journaling. Anything that does not emit light and does not require active thinking.

Now, let your body begin its descent. Soften your face. Let your hands rest open, palms up.

Breathe in slowly through your nose for four seconds. Hold gently for seven seconds. Release through your mouth for eight seconds.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

Your screens have stopped. Your brain knows now that night has begun. Sleep well.
