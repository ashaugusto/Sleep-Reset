This one is for the night you had a drink, fell asleep easily, and then woke at three like a light switch.

If that is what happened, the night is not a mystery and it is not a relapse of your insomnia. It is a rebound, and it is almost mechanical.

Here is the shape of it. Alcohol is a sedative on the way in, so you go down fast and you go down deep. Then your body clears it, and the sedation lifts in the second half of the night while the rebound rises in its place. Deep sleep gets front loaded, dream sleep gets pushed back and then comes in a rush, your heart rate sits higher than usual and your temperature is up. So you surface, fully, several hours before you meant to.

Add the smaller things. You are dehydrated, your bladder is full, and blood sugar is low. All of them push in the same direction.

None of this means the protocol stopped working. It means last night had a chemical in it that the protocol does not control. Tonight the job is the same as always, and tomorrow the job is to not let one drinking night become three bad ones.

So, right now.

Drink some water if you are thirsty, use the bathroom if you need to, and keep the lights low doing both. That is the whole physical part.

[pause 10]

Back in bed, do not chase the sleep you think you are owed. You had four or five hours of heavy sleep and your body knows it, so sleep pressure is genuinely lower than it would be at three on a normal night. Lying there insisting is how a chemical wake turns into a conditioned one.

Set your jaw loose, drop your shoulders, and let the out breath get longer than the in breath. No counting. Just longer out than in.

[pause 20]

If you are wired rather than heavy after twenty minutes or so, get up. Chair, low light, something boring to read, back to bed when your eyes close on their own. Same as any other night. The rules do not change because the cause changed.

[pause 45]

Tomorrow, four things, and they matter more than tonight does.

Normal wake time. Not later. The temptation after a drinking night is to sleep in, and sleeping in is what carries the damage into the next night.

Light and water in the first half hour. Both of them cost nothing.

Caffeine as usual in the morning, and stop by midday. You will want a third and a fourth. That is the rebound talking.

And do not go to bed early to make up for it. Same bedtime, or later.

One last thing worth knowing, said plainly. Alcohol within about three hours of bed is the version of this that costs you the most, and the cost lands in the second half of the night every time. That is not a rule about drinking. It is a rule about timing, and you can use it or not.

Tonight is explained. Close your eyes and let it be a normal night from here.
