Sleep after illness, surgery, or fever is often worse than the illness itself. Your sleep architecture has been disrupted, your nervous system is rebuilding, and your body is sending mixed signals about when to rest.

Here is what your body is going through. During acute illness, fever and inflammation fragment sleep stages, especially REM and deep sleep. Your immune system uses sleep to repair tissue and clear neurotoxins. The first three to five days after symptoms resolve, your sleep continues to feel off, even if you no longer feel sick. This is normal. The system needs time to rebuild.

Two things to avoid during this recovery window.

One, do not try to catch up on missed sleep. Sleeping in late or napping for hours pushes your circadian clock backward and makes the recovery longer. Stick to your usual wake time, even if it feels harsh. The clock recovers faster than missed sleep does.

Two, do not return to caffeine, alcohol, or heavy exercise too quickly. Your nervous system is hypersensitive after illness. A normal coffee feels like an overdose. A normal workout feels like a marathon. Reintroduce gradually over five to seven days.

The third thing is critical. Resume the basics of the protocol on the first night you feel mostly recovered. The anchor wake time. The screen curfew. The stimulus control. Your body wants normality. The protocol provides the framework.

Tonight, your recovery night, do the smallest version of the protocol. Set the wake time alarm. Phone away. Cool, dark room. Nothing more.

Now, let your body do what it knows how to do. Repair. Reset.

Breathe in for four seconds. Hold for seven. Release for eight.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

You are healing. Sleep is part of the work. Sleep well.
