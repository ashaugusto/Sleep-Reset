This one is for the night you wake up with your heart already going.

Not a slow drift into worry. You are awake and the alarm is on before you have a single thought. The thought arrives second, and it arrives looking like a reason.

That order matters, so hold on to it. The body went first. What is running now is a surge that was already scheduled, cortisol and adrenaline climbing towards morning, landing on whatever is unfinished in your life and lighting it up. At three in the morning you have the full stress response and none of the daytime brain that argues back. So everything looks worse, and the worse it looks, the more alert you get.

The thoughts are louder, not truer. You are not going to decide anything tonight, and nothing here needs deciding tonight.

So we do not start with the mind. We start with the chest.

Breathe in through your nose, a normal breath, then on top of it take a second short sip of air through the nose. Then let it all go out through your mouth, slowly, longer than the way in.

Again. Normal breath in, second short sip on top, and a long slow release.

[pause 12]

Again, and this time make the out breath last as long as it can without straining.

[pause 12]

Twice more, on your own.

[pause 30]

That double breath is the fastest way down that does not need you to feel calm first. It works on a racing heart, and it works whether or not you believe it will.

[pause 8]

Now the part that decides how long this lasts.

Do not argue with the thought. Do not check anything to reassure yourself, no phone, no email, no bank app, no symptom search. Reassurance at three in the morning is the strongest fuel this thing has. It works for about ninety seconds and then the question comes back bigger.

Instead, name it once, in one sentence, and hand it to daylight. Say it in your head like this. This is the three AM version of a real thing, and I will look at it at eight.

Then get out of bed if the alert stays. Chair, low light, something over your shoulders, paper and pen. Write it down until the loop goes quiet, and put one line at the bottom, the smallest thing you can do about it tomorrow.

[pause 60]

Then read something boring until your eyes are closing on their own, and go back to bed only then.

Tomorrow, three things. Normal wake time, no matter what tonight gave you. Ten minutes of outdoor light in the first half hour. And the sentence you wrote, looked at properly, in daylight, when you have a brain to look at it with.

One night like this is a spike, not a return. If your chest is doing this most nights, or if the fear is running in the daytime too, that is worth a conversation with a doctor. This is coaching, not medical care, and there is a version of this that is treatable and is not about sleep at all.

For tonight, nothing is required of you. The surge peaks, then it falls. It always falls.
