If you are listening to this at three in the morning, you are awake, your heart is probably racing, and your mind is running. This protocol is built for this exact moment.

Here is what is happening. Around three to four AM, cortisol naturally rises in preparation for waking. If you have any unresolved stress, that cortisol amplifies the emotional weight of your thoughts. Worries that feel manageable during the day feel catastrophic at three AM. This is biology, not reality. The thoughts are louder, not truer.

Do not try to fall back asleep right now. Trying makes it worse. Sleep is a release, not an effort.

Instead, follow this sequence. First, if you have been lying in bed more than fifteen minutes, get out of bed. Move to another room. Keep lights low. The bed must stay associated with sleep.

Second, write down what is in your head. Anything. Pen and paper, not phone. Bullet points, sentences, fragments. The act of writing transfers thoughts out of working memory.

Third, read something boring. A magazine, a manual, a book you have read before. Not stimulating fiction. Not news. Not anything that activates emotional response.

Fourth, when you feel the heaviness of sleepiness return, and only then, go back to bed. Lights stay off. Phone stays away. No clock checking. No assessing how long it has been.

If sleep does not return within twenty minutes, you repeat the loop. The protocol works on the second or third cycle for most people.

Now, in this moment, before you decide to get up or stay in bed, take three slow breaths.

In through your nose for four seconds. Hold for seven. Out through your mouth for eight.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

Three AM is the hardest hour. You will get through this one. Sleep returns.
