This one is for the night your schedule moved. A flight, a shift change, a late event, a clock that went forward, or one weekend where you got up three hours later than usual and paid for it on Sunday.

If that is the night you are having, you are not fighting insomnia right now. You are fighting a clock that is still set to the old time, and clocks move slowly. Roughly an hour a day, and less than that when you are being pushed later than your body prefers.

That is worth knowing at three in the morning, because it changes what a win is. Tonight is not fixable. The next three days are.

So tonight, keep it small.

Do not lie in bed doing arithmetic about the time back home or the hours left before the alarm. Whatever the number is, it does not change the instruction.

Let your jaw come apart, drop your shoulders, and let the out breath run longer than the in breath. Nothing to count.

If you are heavy, stay where you are and let the night do what it does.

If you are wired after twenty minutes or so, get up. Low light, chair, something boring, back to bed when your eyes are closing on their own. Same protocol, new cause.

[pause 30]

Now the part that actually moves a clock, and it is not what happens at night. It is light and it is your wake time.

Pick the wake time you want to live on, the one that belongs to where you are now or to the schedule you are moving into. Set it, and hold it for three days, including the day you feel wrecked. That is the anchor, and everything else is negotiable.

Then use light on purpose.

If you need to wake up earlier than your body wants, get bright light into your eyes as soon as you get up. Outside, ten to twenty minutes, even under cloud. And keep the evening dim, low lamps, screens down, nothing bright in the last hour.

If you need to move later, do the opposite. Keep the morning dim for the first hour and get bright light in the late afternoon and early evening.

That is the whole mechanism. Light in the morning pulls you earlier. Light at night pushes you later. Everything else is detail.

[pause 15]

Two more things, and then I will leave you to the night.

Naps. On a moved schedule, a nap is allowed, but twenty minutes, before three in the afternoon, and never as a substitute for holding the wake time.

And expectations. Give it one day per hour of shift before you judge how it is going. If you flew across four hours, you are looking at about four days, and the fourth is usually fine. Judging it tomorrow is how people conclude the method broke when what actually happened is that they changed time zones.

Tonight, nothing is required. Your clock is moving already, whether or not you sleep. Close your eyes and let it move.
