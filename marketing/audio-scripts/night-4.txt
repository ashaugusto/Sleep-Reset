Welcome to Night 4. Tonight we shape the cave, your physical sleep environment, into a place your brain learns to associate exclusively with rest.

The science is precise. Your core body temperature must drop by about one to two degrees Celsius for sleep to begin. If your bedroom is warm, your body cannot release that heat, and sleep onset is delayed. Studies converge on a sweet spot of sixteen to nineteen degrees Celsius. Cooler than feels comfortable when you walk in. Warm only under the covers.

Light is just as critical. Even small amounts of light, including the standby LED on a TV or the glow of a charger, signal to your brain that morning is approaching. Melatonin production stays suppressed. Sleep stays shallow.

Sound matters less than most people think, but consistency matters more. A steady fan or white noise is far better than a quiet room with occasional disruptions. Your brain monitors for changes, not absolute volume.

So tonight, make four adjustments. Cool the room. Block out every source of light, including window light. Remove any light-emitting device from your line of sight. And add a constant low hum, a fan or noise machine, if your room has unpredictable sounds.

Your bed is now a place that does one thing. It signals sleep.

Let your eyes close. Let your face soften. Notice the weight of your body against the bed.

The cave is dark. The cave is cool. Sleep well.
