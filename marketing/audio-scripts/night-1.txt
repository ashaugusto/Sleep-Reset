Welcome to Night 1 of the Cognitive Shutdown Method. Tonight we begin with the most important rule in all of behavioral sleep medicine: the anchor. Your wake time matters more than your bedtime.

Here is why. Your body runs on two systems that govern sleep. The circadian system tells your brain when it is day or night. The homeostatic system, also called sleep pressure, accumulates the longer you stay awake. Both systems depend on one consistent signal to function properly, and that signal is your wake time.

When you sleep in on weekends, even by one hour, you scramble both systems at once. Cortisol releases at the wrong time. Melatonin shifts. The brain loses its anchor, and Sunday night insomnia becomes inevitable.

So tonight, you set a wake time. Not an ambitious one. A realistic one. The time you actually need to be up on weekdays. And starting tomorrow, you keep that wake time every single day, including weekends, for the next seven nights. This is non-negotiable.

You do not need to change your bedtime yet. Just the wake time. The bedtime will adjust naturally as sleep pressure builds.

Now, before you sleep tonight, let us prepare your body. Find a comfortable position. Let your shoulders drop. Let your jaw soften.

Take a slow breath in through your nose for four seconds. Hold for seven. And release slowly through your mouth for eight seconds.

Once more. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Release for eight.

Your wake time tomorrow is now an anchor. Everything else builds from it. Sleep well.
