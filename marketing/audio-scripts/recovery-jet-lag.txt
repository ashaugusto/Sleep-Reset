Welcome to the Jet Lag Reset protocol. Time zone changes are not a sleep failure. They are a circadian misalignment, and the fix is precise.

Here is the science. Your body runs on a 24-hour internal clock driven by light, melatonin, and core body temperature. A westward flight pushes your clock back. An eastward flight pushes it forward. The clock adjusts about one hour per day on its own, which means a five-hour shift takes five days to resolve. Unless you intervene.

The intervention has two phases.

Phase one, on the day of arrival. Get outdoor sunlight within an hour of your local wake time, even if cloudy. Sunlight is the most powerful clock reset tool we have. Walk for at least fifteen minutes. Avoid sunglasses. The retina needs the signal.

Phase two, three nights in a row. Force your local sleep window. Even if you feel awake at the new local bedtime, get into bed. Even if you feel exhausted at midday, do not nap longer than twenty minutes. Eat meals on the local schedule, not when your stomach tells you. The clock follows light, food, and forced sleep timing.

For most people, three nights of this protocol resolves a six-hour shift completely. Without it, you are looking at a week or more of broken sleep.

Tonight, if you are arriving in a new time zone, here is your final preparation. Set an alarm for tomorrow morning at the local time you intend to use going forward. Close the curtains tightly. Cool the room.

Now, let your body settle. Soften your jaw. Let your shoulders drop.

Breathe in slowly through your nose for four seconds. Hold for seven seconds. Release through your mouth for eight seconds.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Release for eight.

The clock will catch up. Sleep well.
