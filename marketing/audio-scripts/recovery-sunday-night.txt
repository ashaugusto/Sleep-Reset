Sunday night insomnia is the most common form of conditioned sleep anxiety. If you are listening to this, you are likely lying awake on a Sunday night, dreading the week, and wondering why your body refuses to sleep when it knows it needs to.

Here is what is happening. Your brain has learned to associate Sunday evening with anticipation. The anticipation is not always negative. Sometimes it is excitement, sometimes dread, sometimes both. What matters is that the anticipation creates cortisol release, and cortisol blocks the sleep onset cascade.

There are two compounding problems. First, most people sleep in on weekends, which delays the circadian clock by one to two hours. By Sunday night, the body thinks the night is younger than it actually is. Second, the brain runs a mental rehearsal of Monday's tasks, which keeps the prefrontal cortex active when it should be powering down.

The fix for tonight has three parts.

Part one. If you slept in on Saturday and Sunday, accept that tonight will be harder. The clock is shifted. Going to bed earlier than usual will not work. Your body is not tired yet. Stay up until your usual weekday lights-out time, even if it feels late.

Part two. Do your Monday rehearsal on paper, now, before you lie down. Write the three highest-priority tasks for tomorrow. Write what you will do first when you arrive at work. The brain releases tasks once they are externalized.

Part three. Lower expectations for tonight. The pressure to fall asleep makes falling asleep impossible. Tell yourself you are simply resting in bed. Sleep is a bonus, not a goal.

Now, let go of the week that is coming. Tomorrow is tomorrow. Tonight is just rest.

Breathe in for four seconds. Hold for seven. Release through your mouth for eight.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

Monday will arrive whether you sleep or not. Rest is the only thing in your control. Sleep well.
