Welcome to Night 5. Tonight we address the most common reason people with chronic insomnia stay awake at night. The racing mind.

Here is what the research shows. The thoughts that surface at bedtime are not random. They are unprocessed tasks, unresolved worries, anticipated problems. Your brain has been holding them in working memory all day. When you stop the input, finally lying still in the dark, those held thoughts release.

The solution is not to suppress them. Suppression makes them louder. The solution is to give them somewhere to go, before you lie down.

This technique is called constructive worry, and it has the strongest evidence base of any sleep hygiene intervention. Here is the protocol. Fifteen minutes before your intended bedtime, sit at a table with a piece of paper. Divide the paper into two columns. On the left, write everything that is on your mind. Work, relationships, finances, anything pulling at your attention. On the right, write the single next step for each item, no matter how small. The act of writing the next step closes the loop. Your brain releases the thought.

The paper goes into a drawer. The thoughts stay there until morning. They cannot follow you to the bed.

Now, let go of the thoughts you wrote down. They are stored. You do not need to hold them anymore.

Breathe in through your nose for four seconds. Hold for seven. Out through your mouth for eight.

Again. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

One last time. In for four. Hold for seven. Out for eight.

Your mind is empty for the night. Sleep well.
